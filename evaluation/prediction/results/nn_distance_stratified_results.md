# Nearest-neighbour stratified evaluation

Generated by `evaluation/prediction/scripts/nn_distance_analysis.py`.
Predictions: seed 42, `saved_models_v2`, `PRODUCTION` correction profile.

## Why stratify

The 88 evaluation alloys and the 77 alloys behind the system (ML training set
and knowledge-graph contents are the same list) overlap compositionally. Some
evaluation entries are the same alloy under a different vendor name, so a
pooled metric averages recall on alloys the system has effectively seen with
generalisation to alloys it has not. Stratifying by distance separates the two
without discarding any data.

Distance is Euclidean on wt%-normalised compositions, computed with the
production metric (`rag_tools._composition_distance`), so the strata align with
the thresholds the KG anchoring gate applies at run time.

| stratum | range | meaning | alloys |
|---|---|---|---|
| NEAR | d < 2.0 | near-duplicate; typically the same alloy, renamed | 13 |
| MID | 2.0 ≤ d < 4.5 | genuinely distinct, inside the KG anchoring cutoff | 31 |
| FAR | d ≥ 4.5 | beyond the anchoring cutoff; KG can never fire | 44 |

Distances span 0.02 to 20.35 wt%, median 4.53.

## What the strata show

**KG anchoring contributes only on near-duplicates.** Yield-strength MAE with
KG anchoring against ML+physics:

| stratum | YS MAE change |
|---|---|
| NEAR | −29.9% (107.56 → 75.42) |
| MID | −0.2% (76.05 → 75.93) |
| FAR | 0.0% (identical) |

FAR is identical by construction: every FAR alloy is beyond the d = 4.5 cutoff,
so no proposal can be generated. The informative stratum is MID, where
anchoring *is* permitted and the neighbours are genuinely different alloys.
There the effect is −0.2% on YS and −0.4% on EL: not distinguishable from zero.

The pooled −6.0% YS improvement is therefore carried almost entirely by 13
alloys the knowledge graph already contains. The mechanism is retrieval of a
stored measurement, not calibration by compositional analogy.

**Physics corrections behave in the opposite way** — they help most where the
system has least knowledge. EM MAE falls from 13.32 to 7.78 GPa on FAR (−42%)
and from 14.60 to 6.75 on NEAR, but *worsens* on MID (9.92 → 13.35, +35%). The
MID regression is worth a look on its own; it is not caused by the KG.

**Generalisation gap.** R² for the strength properties drops sharply on FAR
(YS 0.875 NEAR / 0.895 MID / 0.656 FAR; UTS 0.930 / 0.886 / 0.644). Whatever
the MAE comparison says, the models order test alloys much less well once they
are compositionally far from the training set.

## Sanity check: is ML-only also memorising?

Comparing ML-only on NEAR against FAR (negative = better on alloys effectively
seen in training):

| scope | property | n NEAR | MAE NEAR | n FAR | MAE FAR | NEAR − FAR |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all classes | YS | 56 | 107.56 | 139 | 122.83 | −12.4% |
| all classes | UTS | 57 | 96.55 | 142 | 149.38 | −35.4% |
| all classes | EL | 61 | 28.53 | 141 | 11.25 | +153.5% |
| all classes | EM | 55 | 14.60 | 184 | 13.32 | +9.7% |
| SSS | YS | 18 | 125.75 | 84 | 111.77 | +12.5% |
| SSS | UTS | 18 | 93.57 | 88 | 137.01 | −31.7% |
| SSS | EL | 18 | 4.17 | 87 | 12.79 | −67.4% |
| SSS | EM | 22 | 4.76 | 121 | 15.15 | −68.5% |
| Precip | YS | 38 | 98.94 | 39 | 152.31 | −35.0% |
| Precip | UTS | 39 | 97.92 | 41 | 178.91 | −45.3% |
| Precip | EL | 43 | 38.73 | 38 | 10.18 | +280.5% |
| Precip | EM | 33 | 21.16 | 58 | 7.49 | +182.7% |

The pooled row is confounded — NEAR has no SC/DS alloys and FAR is 55% SSS — so
the per-class rows carry the argument.

**Tensile strength shows a consistent training-overlap advantage**: UTS is 32%
better on NEAR for SSS and 45% better for Precip, and Precip YS is 35% better.
So the ML baselines in the pooled table are optimistic, and the caveat is real,
not hypothetical.

**The two apparent counter-examples are outlier artefacts, not evidence
against it.** Precip EL is worse on NEAR (+280%) because of 14 points from two
alloys — Haynes 214 and Haynes 263 above 927 °C, with measured elongation of
95–159% in the super-plastic ductility-recovery regime the model systematically
under-predicts. Excluding those 14 points, NEAR EL MAE is 8.78 against 10.18 on
FAR, i.e. NEAR is better, consistent with the strength result. Likewise 74% of
the Precip EM error on NEAR comes from MAR-M 200 alone (10 points, predicted
≈146 GPa against a reported 218 GPa); excluding that alloy gives 7.92 against
7.49 on FAR, essentially equal.

SSS YS (+12.5%) has no such explanation and rests on 18 rows from 5 alloys; it
is the one cell that does not fit the pattern, and is too small to lean on.

## Caveats

- MAE is not comparable across strata in absolute terms: the strata differ in
  class mix and in property ranges. Within-stratum comparisons between methods,
  and within-class comparisons across strata, are the sound ones.
- The `ML+physics+KG` arm applies only proposals tagged `KG_anchoring`. The full
  agent system also receives empirical physics proposals, so its larger gain is
  not attributable to the knowledge graph on the strength of this analysis.
- NEAR has 13 alloys and MID 31; per-class cells within a stratum can fall to
  under 20 rows. Small-sample noise is a real constraint on the finer splits.
- `d < 2.0` is a judgement call for "same alloy". The list above supports it,
  but the boundary is not sharp — Rene 100 / IN-100 at d = 1.60 are related but
  arguably distinct alloys.
