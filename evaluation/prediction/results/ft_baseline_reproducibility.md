# The fine-tuned baseline is no longer reproducible

Generated by `evaluation/prediction/scripts/ft_drift_check.py`.

## What was attempted

The archived GPT-4.1-mini baseline was collected in February 2026 and
scored on NIMONIC PE16's uncorrected composition. Since that error was
fixed for this system's arms and not for the baseline, whichever method
won elastic modulus depended on leaving a known data error in the
competitor's input. The re-run existed to remove that asymmetry.

It did not. The same fine-tuned model id, prompted identically by the
same harness, now returns something categorically different.

## Coverage

| run | rows answered | of |
|---|---:|---:|
| archived (Feb 2026) | 466 | 471 |
| re-run (Aug 2026) | 329 | 471 |

The 142 missing rows are not errors or rate limits.
On each of them the model returned
`{"yield_strength": 0.0, "uts": 0.0, "elongation": 0.0, "elasticity": 0.0}`
or the same shape filled with nulls, on **six** independent draws at
different seeds. A zero yield strength alongside a zero elastic modulus is
not a physical claim, so these are recorded as failures rather than scored
as predictions of zero.

## Output collapse

| property | distinct values, archived | distinct values, re-run |
|---|---:|---:|
| YS | 93 | 13 |
| UTS | 97 | 13 |
| EL | 69 | 15 |
| EM | 58 | 26 |

The re-run answers 93 distinct yield strengths with 13, and one value
covers 42% of rows (730 MPa); a single tensile
strength covers 44% (860 MPa). Three
compositionally unrelated alloys -- MAR-M 247, MAR-M 200 and TRW-NASA VI A
-- now receive identical predictions. The model has largely stopped
conditioning on composition.

## What this means for the paper

**Neither number is publishable as the fine-tuned baseline.**

- The February figures cannot be reproduced by a reviewer, or by us. They
  also carry the PE16 defect.
- The August figures measure a degraded endpoint, not the baseline as it
  was evaluated. Reporting them would flatter this system substantially
  and for the wrong reason -- the same failure mode as scoring a
  competitor on a composition we had already corrected for ourselves.

The fix is to re-fine-tune from the training data committed at
`backend/superalloy_preprocess/output_data/finetuned_data/`, which is
under version control, and evaluate that model. It is the only route to a
baseline a reader can reproduce. Until then the paper should either omit
the fine-tuned baseline or report the February figures with an explicit
statement that the hosted model has since changed and the numbers cannot
be regenerated.

## The control that rules out our own settings

The re-run was seeded at sampling temperature 0.0 for reproducibility,
which is a change from the archived protocol. That change is not the
cause. Reproducing February exactly -- same prompt, temperature 0.3, no
seed -- returns a null answer on 38% of a 45-row sample today, against
none in 466 archived rows. Sampling at temperature 0.0 with a seed gives
42%, and temperature 0.3 with a seed 24%. Every configuration fails; the
archived one fails too.

## One finding survives the drift

The train/test overlap result in `ft_baseline_analysis.md` reproduces on
the degraded model. Even collapsed, it is markedly better on the alloys it
was fine-tuned on -- yield-strength MAE 155.07 against 220.60 on alloys it
never saw. The memorisation signature is a property of the fine-tune, not
an artefact of the February snapshot.
