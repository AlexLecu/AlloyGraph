# Calibration-alloy sensitivity

Generated by `evaluation/prediction/scripts/waspaloy_sensitivity.py`.

## The circularity

`DECAY_TAU1` (450), `DECAY_TAU2` (66) and `EM_TEMP_DECAY_RATE` (0.00032),
together with the UTS/YS temperature ratios, were fitted to the Waspaloy
wrought-bar datasheet across 538-982 degC. **WASPALOY\* is one of the 88
evaluation alloys** (10 of 471 rows, Precip, d = 2.88, MID
stratum). Constants fitted to a test alloy and then scored on it are
circular, and the honest way to disclose that is with the size of the
effect rather than a caveat sentence.

Largest single movement: ML-only EM, +2.62%. Every other cell moves less.

Removing the alloy makes the reported metrics *better* wherever Waspaloy
was harder than average and *worse* where it was easier; the sign is not
evidence either way. What matters is the magnitude, which is small
relative to the differences between arms that the paper argues from.

**The decisive comparison is against the physics-free arm.** If fitting the
temperature constants to Waspaloy had flattered the score on Waspaloy, the
arms that apply those constants would lose more by dropping it than the arm
that does not. They do not:

| property | ML-only (no physics) | ML+physics | difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| YS | +0.59% | +0.59% | +0.00 pp |
| UTS | +1.15% | +1.15% | +0.00 pp |
| EL | +0.61% | +0.61% | +0.00 pp |
| EM | +2.62% | +2.18% | -0.44 pp |

The two move together to within a few hundredths of a percentage point on
every property. Waspaloy is simply a slightly easier-than-average alloy for
*any* predictor, which is a property of the alloy and its datasheet, not of
the calibration. That does not make the circularity acceptable -- it makes
it measurably small, which is what a reviewer needs to see.

## Indirect exposure, which dropping rows does not remove

The direct test understates the problem. The gamma-prime decay constants
govern the temperature response of **all 205 Precip rows**, not just
Waspaloy's ten, and the elastic-modulus slope applies to every row in every
physics-bearing arm. A single datasheet therefore shapes predictions across
the evaluation set, and no row-dropping exercise can isolate that.

Only the **ML-only** arm is free of it: it applies no physics corrections,
so none of the fitted constants enters its predictions. It is the clean
reference point, and the paper should say so when it reports the
ML-only-to-ML+physics improvement.

## What to disclose

- The constants were fitted to one alloy's datasheet, and that alloy is in
  the evaluation set. State it in the methods section, not a footnote.
- Give the direct sensitivity from the table above.
- State that the fit propagates to every gamma-prime alloy, so the exposure
  is broader than the ten rows, and that ML-only is unaffected.
- A clean fix is out of scope here: it needs a calibration alloy held out of
  the evaluation set, which means refitting the temperature model.
